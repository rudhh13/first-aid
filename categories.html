Essential First Aid & Safety Tips

Be prepared to respond to medical emergencies with confidence.

What is First Aid?

First aid is the **immediate assistance** given to someone suffering from an injury or illness before professional medical help arrives. It can be the difference between life and death in emergencies.

Basic Principles of First Aid:

  • **Preserve Life** – Ensure the injured person is breathing and out of immediate danger.
  • **Prevent Condition from Worsening** – Take necessary measures to stop bleeding, immobilize fractures, etc.
  • **Promote Recovery** – Provide comfort and reassurance until medical help arrives.

Common First Aid Situations & Tips

Allergic Reaction 🤧

Steps:

  1. Identify the allergen and remove it if possible.
  2. Give an antihistamine (such as diphenhydramine) if mild symptoms appear.
  3. Help the person stay calm and seated in a comfortable position.
  4. If severe symptoms develop (difficulty breathing, swelling, dizziness), use an epinephrine auto-injector (EpiPen) if available.
  5. Call emergency services immediately if symptoms worsen or do not improve.

Animal Bites

Steps:

  1. Wash the bite wound with soap and water for at least 5 minutes.
  2. Apply an antiseptic solution to disinfect the wound.
  3. Apply gentle pressure with a clean cloth if there is bleeding.
  4. Cover the wound with a sterile bandage.
  5. Monitor for signs of infection, such as redness, swelling, or pus.
  6. Seek medical attention if the bite is deep, from a wild animal, or if rabies vaccination is needed.

Asthma

Steps:

  1. Help the person sit upright and stay calm.
  2. Assist them in using their prescribed inhaler (preferably a reliever inhaler like albuterol).
  3. Encourage slow, deep breaths to ease breathing difficulty.
  4. Loosen any tight clothing around the chest and neck.
  5. Seek emergency medical help if symptoms do not improve within 5-10 minutes or worsen.

Blisters

Steps:

  1. Wash your hands and the affected area with mild soap and water.
  2. Cover the blister with a clean, non-stick bandage to prevent infection.
  3. Avoid popping the blister, as it protects the underlying skin.
  4. If the blister bursts, clean the area and apply antibiotic ointment.
  5. Reduce friction by wearing comfortable, well-fitting shoes or using protective padding.

Burns

Steps:

  1. Cool the burn with cold running water for at least 10 minutes.
  2. Remove tight clothing or jewelry near the burn area.
  3. Cover the burn with a clean, non-stick bandage.
  4. Do not apply butter, ice, or ointments.
  5. Seek medical help if the burn is severe.
